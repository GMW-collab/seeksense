"Assessment" is used to describe many different things, from a ten-minute triage call to several hours with a specialist. This article is about a structured assessment with a mental health practitioner, arranged to understand your difficulties more clearly.

## What an assessment usually covers

A practitioner will usually explore:

- the difficulties you are experiencing now, and how they affect you
- when they started, and what has been happening in your life around that time
- your history, including any previous support or treatment
- your strengths, and what helps
- your safety and wellbeing

Some practitioners use standardised questionnaires alongside the conversation. These can be useful, but they are only one source of information. A questionnaire cannot by itself tell you what is going on.

## Assessment and diagnosis are not the same thing

An assessment can lead to a clearer understanding of your difficulties without a diagnosis. Whether a diagnosis can be given depends on the practitioner's profession, training and registration, and on the condition involved. Some diagnoses can only be made through specialist services.

If a diagnosis matters to you, perhaps for treatment, work or education, ask before you book: *Can this assessment result in a diagnosis, and will it be recognised by my GP or other services?*

## What you should receive

You should know in advance what you will receive at the end. That might be a verbal discussion, a written summary, recommendations for support, or a letter to your GP. Who sees any written report should be your choice.

## Questions to ask before booking

- Who will carry out the assessment, and what is their professional registration?
- How long will it take, and over how many appointments?
- What will I receive at the end?
- Can it include a diagnosis? If not, how could I get one if it is needed?
- What does it cost, and is anything else charged separately?

## Online self-tests

Many websites offer quick questionnaires. They can prompt useful reflection, but they are not an assessment and should not be treated as one. If a result worries you, talk to your GP or a qualified practitioner.
