Most therapy services, including ours, work by appointment. They are not set up to respond quickly in a crisis. If you need help today, these routes are designed for that.

## If a life is at risk

Call **999** or go to your nearest A&E. That includes situations where someone has seriously harmed themselves, taken an overdose, or feels unable to keep themselves safe. The NHS is clear that a mental health emergency should be taken as seriously as a physical one.

## If it is urgent but not life-threatening

In England, Scotland and Wales, call **111 and choose option 2** to reach NHS urgent mental health support, 24 hours a day. In Northern Ireland, call **Lifeline on 0808 808 8000**.

You can also ask your GP surgery for an urgent appointment.

## If you need to talk to someone

- **Samaritans**: call **116 123**, free, any time of day or night.
- **SHOUT**: text **SHOUT** to **85258** for free, confidential text support.

You do not need to be in crisis to contact these services. They are there for anyone who is struggling.

## If you are worried about someone else

The same services can help you work out what to do. If you believe someone is in immediate danger, call 999.

## Afterwards

Once the immediate pressure has eased, it can help to think about longer-term support. Our article on [therapy, assessment and signposting](/resources/therapy-assessment-or-signposting/) explains the options.
