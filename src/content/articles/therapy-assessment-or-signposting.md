When you first look for help, the language can be confusing. Services describe themselves as offering therapy, counselling, assessment, support, guidance or navigation, often without saying how these differ. That confusion can make it harder to ask for the right thing, so here is a plain account of three different kinds of help.

## Talking therapy

Talking therapy is a series of conversations with a trained practitioner, usually at a regular time each week. The aim is change: understanding your difficulties better, finding different ways of responding to them, or working through something painful in a setting that is safe and confidential.

Therapy is a good fit when you have a sense of what you want to work on, or at least a sense that something needs to shift, and you are able to commit to regular sessions for a period of time.

## Assessment

An assessment aims to understand rather than to change. A practitioner gathers a careful picture of your difficulties, their history and their effect on your life, sometimes using established questionnaires. You usually receive a summary and recommendations at the end.

An assessment is useful when you are unsure what is going on, when you need a clearer picture before choosing support, or when you want something in writing to share with your GP. Whether it can include a formal diagnosis depends on who carries it out. Ask about this directly.

## Signposting

Signposting means being pointed towards the services that are most likely to help. It might lead to an NHS service you can refer yourself to, a charity specialising in your situation, a peer support group, or a particular kind of specialist.

Signposting is valuable when the system feels confusing, or when the best help may not be the service you happen to have contacted first.

## How to tell which you need

There is no wrong place to start. A useful question to ask yourself is: *do I mainly want to understand what is going on, to work on it, or to find out where to go?* Many people need a little of each, in that order.

If you are not sure, an initial conversation with a practitioner is designed for exactly this. A good practitioner will tell you honestly if what you are asking for is not what you need.
