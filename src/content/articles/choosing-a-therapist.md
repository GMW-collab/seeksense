Choosing a therapist is a personal decision, and the right fit matters. It also helps to know how the profession is organised in the UK, because not every title means what you might expect.

## Some titles are protected, others are not

Certain psychologist titles are protected by law. Anyone using titles such as *clinical psychologist*, *counselling psychologist* or *practitioner psychologist* must be registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC). The HCPC publishes a register you can search.

The plain title *psychologist* is not protected on its own. Neither are *counsellor* or *psychotherapist*. That does not mean counsellors and psychotherapists are unqualified. Many are highly trained and belong to professional bodies with their own standards, but the title alone does not tell you that.

## Check for an accredited register

The NHS advises that if you see a private therapist, you should check that they are registered with a professional organisation accredited by the Professional Standards Authority (PSA). Accredited registers set standards for training and conduct and have a process for complaints.

Most registers have an online search. Checking takes a couple of minutes and is a normal thing to do. A good practitioner will expect it.

## Questions worth asking

Qualifications tell you part of the story. These questions often tell you more:

- **What kind of therapy do you offer, and why might it suit me?** You are looking for a clear answer in everyday language.
- **How will we know if it is helping?** Good practitioners review progress with you.
- **What are the limits of confidentiality?** There should be a clear, unhurried explanation.
- **Do you have clinical supervision?** Regular supervision is standard good practice.
- **What happens if I want to stop?** You should never feel trapped in therapy.
- **What are your fees and cancellation terms?** These should be in writing before you start.

## Trust how it feels

After a first session, ask yourself whether you felt listened to and treated with respect. It is reasonable to try a different practitioner if the fit does not feel right.

## Free and low-cost options

You may be able to refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies for problems such as anxiety and depression, without going through your GP. Some employers, universities and charities also offer free therapy. Private therapy is one option among several.
