## What it involves

Therapy starts with an [initial consultation](/services/initial-consultation/), where we agree whether working together makes sense. If it does, we meet regularly at the same time. The first few sessions are about understanding what is going on and what you would like to be different. After that, the work follows what matters to you.

We review together how things are going, usually every six sessions or so. You can raise doubts at any point. Saying "I'm not sure this is helping" is a useful part of therapy, not a failure of it.

## Our approach

No single type of therapy suits everyone. [[verify:practitioner: Description of the practitioner's therapeutic approach | The practitioner draws on [named approaches], adapting to what each person finds useful.]] We will explain what we are doing and why, and we will not push you towards conversations you are not ready for.

## Confidentiality

What you say in sessions stays between you and your therapist. There are a few exceptions, such as a serious risk of harm to you or someone else, or where the law requires it. We explain these exceptions clearly at the start, so nothing comes as a surprise. Like all responsible practitioners, your therapist discusses their work in confidence with a clinical supervisor, without sharing identifying details.

## Ending

You can stop whenever you choose. Where possible, we plan endings together so you leave with a clear sense of what has changed and what to do if things become difficult again.
